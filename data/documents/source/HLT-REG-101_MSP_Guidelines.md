# Medicare Secondary Payer (MSP) Guidelines

**Document ID:** HLT-REG-101  
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2025  
**Jurisdiction:** United States  
**Authority Status:** Current

---

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes guidelines for identifying when Medicare is the **secondary payer** and should not be the primary payer for services rendered.

---

## 2. When Medicare is Secondary

Medicare is secondary when the member has other primary insurance coverage, including:

| **Primary Payer Type** | **Example** |
|---|---|
| Employer Group Health Plan | Active employment (employer 20+ employees) |
| COBRA Coverage | Continuing coverage after leaving employment |
| Auto Insurance | No-fault or medical payments coverage |
| Workers' Compensation | Work-related injury or illness |
| Veterans Affairs (VA) Benefits | Eligible veterans |
| TRICARE | Active duty military and families |
| Other Health Insurance (OHI) | Spouse's employer plan |

---

## 3. Determining Primary vs Secondary

### For Members with Employer Coverage

- **Employer ≤ 100 employees:** Medicare pays primary
- **Employer > 100 employees:** Employer plan pays primary

### For Disabled Members

- **Employer ≤ 100 employees:** Medicare pays primary
- **Employer > 100 employees:** Employer plan pays primary

### For ESRD (End-Stage Renal Disease)

- **First 30 months:** Employer plan pays primary
- **After 30 months:** Medicare pays primary

---

## 4. Claim Submission Instructions

When Medicare is secondary:

1. Submit claim to primary payer first.
2. Wait for primary payer's payment or denial.
3. Submit the claim to Medicare with the primary payer's EOB (Explanation of Benefits).
4. Medicare will make a secondary payment, up to the remaining allowed amount.

---

## 5. Penalties for Non-Compliance

- Interest charges on delayed payments
- Potential liability for payments made in error
- Civil monetary penalties up to $10,000 per claim
