CLAIMS APPEALS PROCESS FAQ
===========================

Document ID: HLT-FAQ-003
Effective Date: November 15, 2025
Version: v1.0
Authority Status: Current

Q1: How do I file an appeal?

Submit a written request within 60 days of the denial notice. Include:

- Claim ID number
- Member ID number
- Date of service
- Reason for appeal
- Supporting documentation (clinical notes, etc.)

Send to:
Claims Appeals Department
P.O. Box 5555
City, State 12345

Q2: What is the timeframe for appeal decisions?

- Standard appeals: 30 days
- Urgent appeals (life-threatening): 72 hours
- Expedited appeals: 7 days

Q3: What documentation is required?

- Clinical notes
- Prior authorization documents
- Medical records
- Physician statements
- Lab results or imaging reports

Q4: What happens if the appeal is denied?

Members may request an external review by an independent review organization (IRO). The IRO decision is binding on the payer but not on the member.

Q5: Can I appeal a partial denial?

Yes. Partial denials can be appealed for the denied portion only. The approved portion will be paid.

Q6: Can my provider file an appeal on my behalf?

Yes. Providers may file appeals with member authorization. Providers must submit the same documentation.

Q7: What are common denial reasons?

- Not medically necessary
- Experimental/investigational
- Not covered under plan
- Prior authorization not obtained
- Out-of-network services
- Missing documentation
- Timely filing deadline missed

Q8: How many levels of appeal are available?

Level 1: Internal Appeal (payer review)
Level 2: Internal Appeal (second-level review)
Level 3: External Review (independent review organization)

Q9: What if I disagree with the external review decision?

External review decisions are binding on the payer. Members may pursue legal action if still dissatisfied.
